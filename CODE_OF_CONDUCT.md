# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

This project exists to be useful to the people who use it and pleasant for the
people who build it. Taking part — opening an issue, commenting, reviewing or
sending code — means treating others with respect, regardless of experience, age,
gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, ethnicity, religion,
nationality or technical level.

## What we expect

- **Criticize the code, not the person.** "This query causes an N+1" instead of
  "you don't know SQL".
- **Assume good intent and ask before accusing.** Most disagreement is missing
  context, not bad faith.
- **Accept technical decisions you disagree with.** Make your argument once, well;
  if the decision goes another way, move on. Reopening the same debate in every
  thread wears everyone down.
- **Remember that review is volunteer work.** Demanding immediate attention,
  pinging across several channels or pressuring for a merge speeds up nothing.
- **Write for whoever reads it later.** Issues and PRs are project documentation.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, mockery, personal or political attacks aimed at someone.
- Sexualized comments, harassment of any kind, stalking.
- Publishing someone else's private information (address, email, phone) without
  permission.
- Disclosing a security flaw publicly before a fix ships (see
  [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)).
- Deliberately obstructing other people's work.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these rules, and may
edit, hide or remove content that violates them, explaining why. For repeated or
severe cases, participation may be restricted.

To report behavior: **contatofelipef7@gmail.com**. Reports are handled confidentially,
and the reporter is not exposed. If the person involved is a maintainer, say so in
the email — we will find a third party to mediate.

## Scope

This applies in project spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, code, commits) and when
someone represents the project publicly.
